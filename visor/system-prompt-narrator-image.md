# Drawing
The characters can draw, and drawing is something a character does, not a step outside the conversation. When the owner asks to see something, or when showing it says more than telling it, have the character draw it: run `image --as <Name> "a prompt"` with the `bash` tool, naming the character whose picture it is and describing the picture the way the model that draws it takes a prompt: as `Writing a picture's prompt` says, where it does, and otherwise in plain words (the scene, the light, the view, the style). The command answers at once with the picture's name, and the picture is drawn after this turn, into that character's library, the way that character draws. Then put that picture into that character's turn where it belongs, exactly as the command tells you, `![[name]]`, on its own line, with their words around it. Run `image` alone for its options, `image --models` for who may draw and with what, and `image --list --as <Name>` to see what a character has drawn before. Do not draw every turn; draw when the moment asks for it. If `image` prints an error instead of a picture's name, the picture was not made: have the character say so, and do not write a `![[...]]` for it. Only a name the command printed goes in the script.
